1.2/The eight phases of commercial litigation

One matter, eight phases — each with its own pressure point.

01

Investigation / pre-suit

"The case just landed. I need to get up to speed on the facts and the law — fast."

In: demand letter, client-provided facts
Out: liability assessment, response strategy

02

Pleadings

"I'm drafting in Word while my evidence lives somewhere else entirely."

In: facts, legal research
Out: complaint / answer, motions

03

Early case assessment

"The complaint's filed. Now I gather everything the client has and decide what to ask for in discovery."

In: filed complaint, client information
Out: discovery requests, case strategy

04

Discovery

"Missing one document in this pile could end my career."

In: document populations
Out: reviewed docs, privilege log

05

Dispositive + expert motions

"Connecting expert opinions to live evidence is all manual work."

In: expert reports
Out: Daubert motions, summary judgment

06

Pre-trial

"I'm picking exhibits from thousands of docs with no unified view."

In: full case record
Out: exhibits, witness prep, drafts

07

Trial

"I need the right exhibit right now while testimony is running live."

In: exhibits, live testimony
Out: verdict, trial record

08

Post-trial

"Everything I learned on this case lives only in my head."

In: trial record, verdict
Out: findings of fact, appeal notice

The path isn't always one-way. In complex cases litigators loop back — continuing discovery, re-investigating, or filing new pleadings mid-case. Done by hand, revisiting a phase is slow — exactly where one unified record earns its keep.

Adoption is real, and it's uneven.

Grounded in Kaiser and Auto Review research

$500M law-firm investment in AI in 2026 — the spend is already flowing into the practice
Persona What is changing today The remaining friction
AAmanda Actively piloting insourcing. Early-case-assessment tools make it feasible to bring more work in-house. Data security and outside-counsel resistance remain barriers. ROI is hard to defend to the CFO.
BBill The AI vendor landscape is expanding rapidly — more options, more evaluation work. Every new tool is a new integration, data risk, and line item. Fragmentation is getting worse first.
JJose Case summaries in minutes, not days. AI surfaces key documents without manual associate synthesis. Still can't verify facts independently in real time. Trust threshold not met for strategic decisions.

BBo AI review surfaces hot documents faster. Deposition-outline drafting is beginning to be AI-assisted. Can't always explain why the AI flagged something. Defensibility of AI work product is unresolved.
TTanner AI triage is reducing the volume of documents he must manually click through. Genuinely uncertain his role exists in three years. AI is both his relief and his existential threat.
VVishal AI culling and early case assessment shrink the data he stages for review, and productions are getting AI-assisted QC. More tools mean more places the same data can drift. He is accountable when an AI-assisted production is wrong.
EErin Clients are asking what AI touched their matter, and vendors are bundling review, hosting, and analytics into one platform. She owns discovery strategy but splits the platform decision with the CIO's office — so consolidation moves slowly.

3.2/The insourcing movement

A structural shift already underway.

Kaiser Associates 2025

Corporate legal

Pressing for predictability

Clients are pressuring outside counsel for cost predictability and AI-driven efficiency. In-house teams handle early case assessments and initial reviews themselves before escalating. The Associate General Counsel's story is this market shift in human form — under CFO pressure, with AI finally giving her the tools to insource.

Law firms

Bringing capability in-house

Third-party managed review demand in the US is 15-25% lower than 2024 levels, driven by rising external costs, improving in-house technology capabilities, and a shift toward smaller, more frequent matters that internal teams can handle directly. In-house review capability is projected to grow approximately 10% between 2025 and 2028 as firms invest in internal teams and platforms.

▪Corporate in-house teams are the most likely near-term adopters of unified litigation platforms.

▪The platforms that win will be the ones that support this shift rather than resist it.

3.3/The trust threshold

Where AI adoption is — and isn't — happening.

HAPPENING

✓Document review and triage
✓Timeline and chronology generation
✓Background research on witnesses and entities
✓Deposition-outline drafting (early adoption)
✓Case summarization for partners

Not yet

✕Autonomous drafting of high-stakes filings
✕Strategic case recommendations
✕Settlement analysis and recommendations
✕Work product requiring court-defensible citation
✕Any output a partner must sign their name to

Practitioners need to explain why the AI flagged something before they'll act on it. The line is moving — but it hasn't moved as far as the vendor hype suggests.

4.2/Intel brief — the AI hotshots

Building from the top of the workflow down.

Harvey

Touches Across the lifecycle — except discovery (doesn't run productions)

Their play

Entered through legal drafting and research. Now in 60+ Am Law 100 firms with deep Lexis integration. Moving aggressively into deposition prep and case strategy.

Their vulnerability

An overlay. Relies on users extracting data from other systems. Can't securely host enterprise-scale document populations. No defensible production logs.

Legora

Touches Across the lifecycle — except discovery; only a toehold there via Everlaw

Their play

Entered through collaboration and workflow. Valued at $5B+. Has acquired three companies and integrated with Everlaw — a toehold in the eDiscovery layer.

Their vulnerability

Still primarily a drafting and collaboration tool. The Everlaw integration is new and unproven at scale. Lacks document infrastructure to ground AI in defensible facts.

Claude (Anthropic)

Touches Embedded inside others (Relativity aiR, connectors) — not a standalone tool

Their play

Building an agentic OS for knowledge work. MCP connectors span eDiscovery and legal research. Relativity has integrated Claude into aiR for Case Strategy.

Their vulnerability

General-purpose, not litigation-specific. No native document layer, no legal data provenance, no court-defensible output. Strength is being adopted as infrastructure by others.

4.3/Intel brief — the classic competitors

Building from eDiscovery up.

Relativity

Their play

Filed its S-1. Shipped aiR for Case Strategy with Claude integration. Repositioning from eDiscovery incumbent to legal-intelligence platform. Dominant install base in large law firms.

Their vulnerability

Legacy technical architecture. AI implementations are add-ons with per-run pricing that creates the budget unpredictability corporate buyers like Amanda actively avoid. No meaningful move into legal research or full-lifecycle strategy.

Everlaw

Their play

Partnered with Legora, gaining a collaboration and drafting layer on top of its eDiscovery core. Positioned as a more modern alternative to Relativity with a cleaner UX.

Their vulnerability

The Legora partnership creates dependency rather than ownership. Still primarily an eDiscovery platform. Pricing model and enterprise security posture remain concerns for large corporate buyers.

Both build from the eDiscovery layer upward — the mirror image of the AI-native startups. Neither has yet assembled the full, defensible, commercially viable picture.

4.4/Intel brief — the LSPs

Service businesses trying to become software businesses.

Epiq · Consilio · Lighthouse · and others

The legal service providers

Their play

Historically the recipients of outsourced legal work, LSPs are repositioning as AI-enabled managed-service providers. The evolved pitch: "AI is complicated to stand up yourself — let us run it for you." They wrap software around existing headcount to preserve margins, offering a lower-risk entry point for firms hesitant to run AI internally.

Their vulnerability

Generative AI directly threatens the headcount-driven revenue model they're built on. Managed review — the core of most LSP businesses — is already down 20–25% since 2024. They lack a native SaaS platform identity, and as firms and corporates get comfortable running AI themselves, the LSP value proposition erodes. Compounding this: clients are increasingly unwilling to pay legacy rates for work AI now performs — squeezing the billable hours the entire model depends on.
